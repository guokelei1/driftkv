# Core Writing Framework: At-the-Desk Quick Reference

## 1. One-Sentence Overall Framework

```text
Field/background -> importance -> current knowledge -> specific gap/problem
-> research aim/method -> evidence/results -> interpretation/comparison
-> limitations/boundaries -> achievement/contribution -> applications/future direction
```

This is not the only section arrangement, but the functional chain that every paper must be able to close.

## 2. Answer These Before You Start Writing

- What is the central claim of this paper? Write it in one sentence; do not write the title first.
- What does this study resolve a lack of: object, data, mechanism, method, comparison, precision or application evidence?
- What is the most important achievement of this paper?
- What is the contribution of this paper to existing knowledge or reality?
- Which result genuinely supports the central claim?
- Which conclusions are merely interpretations, speculations or possible applications?
- Who are the target readers? What background can be assumed, and what cannot?
- How do recent accepted papers at the target venue arrange the title, abstract, section skeleton, figures and evaluation?

## 3. Minimum Functional Skeleton for Each Part

> Genre note: this project writes CS systems conference papers. The skeletons below are the
> systems-paper versions; the section-to-book mapping and full rules are in
> [guidelines/14-系统论文结构与体裁.md](guidelines/14-系统论文结构与体裁.md).

### Introduction

```text
Establish the workload/technology pressure that makes the problem real now
-> Show why existing systems fail at it (one or two sentences on closest work)
-> State the key insight in one plain sentence
-> Introduce the system: name, what it does, its 2-3 core mechanisms
-> Contribution bullets (3-5, each verifiable in the Evaluation)
-> Results preview with concrete numbers and evaluative direction
```

### Background & Motivation

```text
Minimum background the reader needs (workload, hardware, prior-system behavior)
-> Your own motivating measurements quantifying the problem
-> Non-obvious/negative findings that rule out simple fixes
-> Distilled requirements or observations (R1..Rn) that the design must satisfy
-> Optional observation-to-design mapping table
```

### Overview

```text
Define the core abstractions (controlled terminology, unit of work, guarantee)
-> Architecture: components, each with input/output/single responsibility
-> Narrow interfaces between components
-> Life of a request/job: one end-to-end walkthrough naming design sections
```

### Design (each of the 2-3 design sections)

```text
Restate the specific problem, echoing its requirement from Motivation
-> Key insight in one sentence, before any formalism
-> Mechanism: data structures, equations, protocol, pseudocode
-> Correctness, failure and boundary behavior; fallback path
-> Honest costs where they are incurred
```

### Implementation

```text
Stack, languages, LOC (core vs tests), built vs reused
-> 2-4 non-obvious engineering points a re-implementer needs
```

### Evaluation

```text
Research questions RQ1..RQn tied to the contribution bullets
-> Setup: hardware, workloads, baselines (with tuning stated), metrics, statistics protocol
-> One subsection per RQ, each opening with its one-sentence answer
-> End-to-end vs strongest baselines; ablation per design section; scaling; overheads
-> Honest attribution of every speedup (baseline, endpoint, residency)
-> Negative results and limitations (Discussion may be folded in here)
```

### Related Work and Conclusion

```text
Related Work near the end, organized by axis of comparison,
  each cluster closed with the measured or precise difference from this paper
-> Conclusion: short; what was built, key insight, headline numbers, take-home message
```

## 4. Minimum Check for Each Paragraph and Sentence

### Paragraph

- Does this paragraph have only one central function?
- Does the first line tell the reader the topic, action or direction?
- Do the subsequent sentences all drive that function?
- Does the end of the paragraph hand the new information to the next paragraph?

### Sentence

- What action does this sentence perform here?
- Is there evidence, basis or scope limitation next to the claim?
- Does the start of the sentence pick up the given information from the previous sentence?
- Is the new information explicit enough to become the given starting point of the next sentence?
- Does the tense express the intended factual strength and temporal relation?
- Can the reader tell who performed the action?
- Does the sentence start with -ing or a preposition? (If so, rewrite it.)
- Do the modal verbs avoid high-risk traps (must = certainty without evidence, can = three meanings, can not ≠ cannot)?
- Do key numbers have an evaluative direction, rather than being "naked numbers"?

## 5. 10-Minute Pre-Submission Check

1. Does the Title predict the real content and results, rather than retaining the working title?
2. Can the Abstract be understood independently of the main text?
3. Is the Introduction's gap answered in the Results/Discussion?
4. Does every research question or hypothesis have a corresponding result?
5. Does every main conclusion have sufficiently strong design and evidence?
6. Is there narrative before and after each graphic, rather than it appearing in isolation?
7. Have the Results' "findings" not been secretly written as facts by the Discussion's "interpretation"?
8. Are limitations stated where they first appear and handled at the end?
9. Is terminology consistently the same throughout, and are pronouns, tense and prepositions unambiguous?
10. If the reader reads only the Title, Abstract, Discussion/Conclusion, can they still know the value of the research?
11. When the Discussion revisits earlier text, does it use the same vocabulary (echo principle) rather than swapping synonyms?
12. Does the strength of implication statements match the evidence (neither too strong nor too weak)?
13. Is the Conclusion distilled from the main text rather than copied from the Abstract?
14. Does every contribution bullet in the Introduction have a matching research question and evidence in the Evaluation?
15. Does every reported speedup name its baseline, endpoint and residency conditions?
