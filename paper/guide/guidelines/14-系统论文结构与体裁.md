# 14 Systems-Paper Genre and Structure (CS Systems Conferences)

> **Precedence note.** This document defines the primary genre for this project: full papers at CS
> systems conferences (SOSP, OSDI, NSDI, ATC, EuroSys, FAST, VLDB-systems, MLSys and similar).
> Where a rule here conflicts with the journal/IMRaD conventions distilled from the original book
> (guidelines 02–08, 11–12), **this document wins on structure, section naming, ordering and
> length**; the book-derived rules continue to win on sentence-level language, ownership, tense,
> evaluative direction and linking (guidelines 09, 10, 13), which are genre-independent.
>
> Source: these rules are NOT from the original book. They are reverse-engineered from strong
> recent systems papers (e.g. vLLM/SOSP'23, Orca/OSDI'22, DistServe/OSDI'24, HCache/EuroSys'24,
> and this project's approved manuscript skeleton). Treat them as descriptive conventions to be
> re-calibrated against fresh target papers for each submission, per NAR-04.

## 1. How a systems reviewer actually reads

A program-committee reviewer typically has 20–40 minutes per paper and reads in this order:
Title → Abstract → Introduction (especially the contribution bullets and result numbers) →
figures and tables → Evaluation headline results → skim Design → Related Work. Two consequences:

1. The Introduction must carry the entire case: problem, why now, key insight, what was built,
   and the headline numbers. A reviewer who is not convinced by page 2 rarely recovers.
2. Every major claim must be findable as a number in a table/figure within the Evaluation.
   Claims without adjacent evidence read as marketing.

## 2. The canonical skeleton

### SYS-01 Use the standard systems skeleton unless target papers show otherwise

- Rule strength: NORMALLY (strong default; deviations need a reason).
- Skeleton:

```text
1  Introduction            problem -> insight -> system -> contributions -> results preview
2  Background & Motivation quantified evidence that the problem is real; derived requirements
3  Overview                abstractions -> architecture -> life of a request/job
4  Design 1                first core mechanism (usually the key insight made concrete)
5  Design 2                second core mechanism
6  Design 3 (optional)     third core mechanism; 2–3 design sections total
7  Implementation          stack, LOC, non-obvious engineering, what is reused vs built
8  Evaluation              RQ-driven; setup -> end-to-end -> per-mechanism -> overheads
   (8.x Discussion & limitations often folded in here)
9  Related Work            comparative positioning, usually near the end
10 Conclusion              short; take-home message
```

- Key differences from the journal/IMRaD model in guidelines 02–06:
  - There is no section called "Methods" or "Results". The book's Methods rules map onto
    **Design + Implementation**; its Results rules map onto **Evaluation**.
  - **Related Work goes near the end**, not inside the Introduction. The Introduction cites
    closest prior work in one or two sentences only, to sharpen the gap.
  - **Discussion is usually not a separate section.** Fold interpretation, limitations and
    scope into the Evaluation (a final "Discussion and limitations" subsection) or a short
    standalone section before Related Work.
  - A systems paper has **2–3 named core design points, not many small ones**. If you have six
    mechanisms, group them under two or three design sections; scattered mechanisms without an
    overview is the single most common structural failure.
- Completion criterion: reading only the section headings, a reviewer can state what the system
  is, its 2–3 core ideas, and where the evidence lives.

### SYS-02 The Introduction ends with contribution bullets and a results preview

- Rule strength: MUST.
- Writing action: after the problem/insight/system narrative (the book's I1–I4 still applies as
  the underlying logic), close the Introduction with:
  1. an explicit contribution list ("This paper makes the following contributions:") of 3–5
     bullets, each naming a mechanism or finding plus, where possible, its headline number;
  2. a one-paragraph summary of the main experimental results with concrete numbers and
     evaluative direction (e.g. "reduces update cost to only 0.12× of recomputation while
     recovering 0.59 of the quality gap");
  3. optionally a one-sentence roadmap ("The remainder of this paper...") — many venues omit it;
     follow target papers.
- The contributions must be verifiable: each bullet should correspond to a design section and to
  at least one table/figure in the Evaluation. Do not list "we conduct extensive experiments" as
  a contribution.
- Completion criterion: a reviewer reading only the bullets can reconstruct the paper's claims
  and knows which numbers to check.

### SYS-03 Motivation must be measured, and must end in requirements

- Rule strength: MUST for the Background & Motivation section.
- Writing action:
  1. establish the minimum background the reader needs (workload, hardware, prior-system
     behavior) — background is what the reader must know, not everything the authors know;
  2. present **motivating measurements**: your own quantified evidence (profiles, traces,
     microbenchmarks) that the problem is real and large. A motivation built only on citations
     is weak; a motivation built on your own numbers is strong;
  3. distill the measurements into explicit **requirements or observations** (R1…Rn / O1…On)
     that the design sections later answer one-for-one;
  4. optionally close with an observation→design mapping table ("Observation O2 → Design §5").
- Negative or non-obvious motivating results (e.g. "the obvious heuristic does not work")
  belong here and strengthen the paper; do not hide them.
- Completion criterion: every design decision later in the paper can be traced back to a
  numbered requirement or measured observation in this section.

### SYS-04 The Overview section is mandatory and does three jobs

- Rule strength: MUST.
- A systems paper without an overview reads as a bag of disconnected mechanisms. The Overview:
  1. **Abstractions**: define the paper's controlled nouns (the objects the system manipulates,
     the unit of work, the contract/guarantee) before any mechanism. Fix the terminology here
     and never use synonyms afterwards (echo principle, DISC-05b).
  2. **Architecture**: one figure plus one paragraph per component stating its input, output and
     single responsibility, and the narrow interfaces between components ("the compiler and the
     engine communicate only through the immutable program artifact").
  3. **Life of a request/job**: an end-to-end walkthrough of one concrete operation through all
     components, in temporal order, naming the sections where each step is detailed. This is the
     reader's map for the design sections.
- Completion criterion: after the Overview, the reader can predict what each design section will
  contain and how the components compose.

### SYS-05 Each design section sells one idea, insight first

- Rule strength: NORMALLY.
- Internal order of a design section:
  1. restate the specific problem this mechanism solves (one or two sentences, echoing the
     requirement from §Motivation);
  2. state the **key insight** in one plain sentence before any formalism;
  3. develop the mechanism (equations, data structures, protocol, pseudocode — pseudocode or a
     numbered protocol is worth more than prose for anything stateful);
  4. handle the hard cases: correctness argument, failure/boundary behavior, and what happens
     when the mechanism's assumption breaks (fallback path);
  5. state costs honestly (memory, extra passes, constants) where they are incurred, not only
     in the Evaluation (mirrors METH-07).
- Alternatives considered and rejected belong in the design section with one sentence of reason,
  or in the Evaluation as an ablation; do not silently ignore the obvious alternative — the
  reviewer will think of it.
- Completion criterion: each design section can be summarized as "problem → insight → mechanism
  → guarantee/limit" in four sentences.

### SYS-06 Implementation is a short, concrete, credibility section

- Rule strength: NORMALLY.
- Contents: languages/frameworks, lines of code (core vs tests), what was built vs reused,
  and 2–4 non-obvious engineering points that a re-implementer would need (memory layout,
  concurrency structure, kernel-launch strategy, serialization format). Nothing conceptual —
  concepts belong in Design.
- Half a page to one page is typical. This section plus the artifact is the systems equivalent
  of the book's reproducibility mandate (METH-02).

### SYS-07 The Evaluation is organized by research questions

- Rule strength: MUST.
- Writing action:
  1. open with 3–5 explicit research questions (RQ1…RQn), each tied to a claim from the
     Introduction's contribution bullets;
  2. then an **experimental setup** subsection: hardware (exact GPU/CPU/NIC/SSD models),
     software versions, workloads/datasets, baselines with tuning effort stated, metrics, and
     the measurement/statistics protocol (repetitions, seeds, variance reporting);
  3. then one subsection per RQ, each opening with a one-sentence answer ("SystemName reduces X
     by Y× (Figure n)") followed by the evidence and its boundaries.
- Required evidence categories for a full paper (include those that apply, and say why if one
  is absent): end-to-end comparison against the strongest baselines; **ablation** attributing
  gains to each design section; **scaling** (nodes/GPUs/data size); **overheads and costs**
  (memory, build/compile time, amortization); sensitivity to key parameters; failure/robustness
  behavior if the paper claims it.
- Baseline discipline: compare against the nearest published competitor(s), a well-tuned naive
  approach, and (if claiming optimality of a mechanism) the mechanism-off configuration.
  A weak or untuned baseline is the most common review kill.
- Completion criterion: every contribution bullet has an RQ; every RQ subsection leads with its
  answer; no headline claim relies on evidence outside the Evaluation.

### SYS-08 Attribute every speedup honestly

- Rule strength: MUST.
- Every reported ratio must name: the baseline, its tuning status, the endpoint of measurement,
  and the residency/placement conditions ("11.2× vs tuned BF16 exact recomputation, identical
  destination boundary, both HBM-resident"). Never let a memory-placement difference masquerade
  as a kernel speedup; never compare against an obviously unoptimized configuration without
  saying so.
- Report negative and neutral results (a layout that did not help, a heuristic that failed);
  systems reviewers reward measured honesty and punish suspicious perfection (extends RES-07).
- All numbers keep the book's evaluative-direction rule (RES-04/RES-08): "only 0.12×",
  "as much as 23.1%".

### SYS-09 Related Work positions comparatively, near the end

- Rule strength: NORMALLY.
- Organize by axis of comparison (not by paper): for each cluster of prior work, one or two
  sentences of what it does, then one sentence of the precise difference from this paper —
  ideally a difference the Evaluation has measured. Run-in bold paragraph headers
  (**Same-model K/V management.**) are the common style; a comparison table is optional but
  effective when there are ≥4 close systems.
- If a close competitor exists, the strongest form of related work is an experimental comparison
  in the Evaluation; the Related Work paragraph then cites that experiment instead of arguing
  verbally (this replaces the declarative-differentiation habit of journal writing).
- The book's INTRO-03 "research map" logic still governs coherence: the section must read as a
  map with this paper's position on it, not a defensive list.

### SYS-10 Figures and page budget

- Rule strength: NORMALLY.
- A strong 12–14-page systems paper typically has 8–12 figures/tables, and **most of them are
  data figures** (bar/line/CDF/Pareto), not architecture diagrams. One or two architecture
  figures suffice; every design claim of importance deserves a data figure.
- Typical page budget (12-page double-column body): Intro 1–1.5, Motivation 1–1.5, Overview 1,
  Design 3–4, Implementation 0.5, Evaluation 4–5, Related 0.75, Conclusion 0.25. Evaluation is
  the largest section; if it is not, the paper is under-evidenced.
- All figure-narrative rules of guideline 11 (introduce, guide, interpret) apply unchanged.

### SYS-11 Abstract and Title follow conference conventions

- Rule strength: NORMALLY.
- Abstract: one paragraph, typically 150–250 words within the venue limit; function order is
  problem/pressure → why existing systems fail → key insight → system name and what it does →
  2–4 concrete result numbers with evaluative direction → scope/availability. All ownership and
  independence rules from guideline 07 apply.
- Title: the dominant convention is `SystemName: What It Does For Whom`
  (e.g. "SystemName: Mechanism for Target Workload").
  A memorable system name doubles as the paper's handle in reviews and citations. All noun-chain
  and promise-fulfilment rules from guideline 08 apply.

### SYS-12 Write for the rebuttal and the artifact from day one

- Rule strength: OPTIONAL STRATEGY (recommended for this project).
- Maintain alongside the draft: a claim→evidence matrix (every numbered claim mapped to the
  table/figure and the result file that produces it); pre-drafted answers to the predictable
  attacks ("why not just recompute", "baseline too weak", "scale too small", "single seed");
  and an artifact plan (one-command reproduction of the main tables, a <30-minute
  "kick-the-tires" configuration, license checks for data/checkpoints).
- Statements of scope ("we do not address X") placed in Overview/Discussion pre-empt reviewer
  attacks more cheaply than rebuttal text.

## 3. Mapping the book's rules onto the systems skeleton

| Book-derived rule set | Where it applies in a systems paper | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| INTRO I1–I4 (guideline 02) | Introduction | Applies; add contribution bullets + results preview (SYS-02); move the research map to Related Work |
| METH-01..07 (guideline 03) | Design + Implementation | Overview-before-detail, rationale-for-choices, ownership, honest problems all apply; METH-02b (lab equipment spatial language) does not apply; "reproducibility" is delivered by Implementation + artifact |
| RES-01..15 (guideline 04) | Evaluation | All apply; organize by RQ (SYS-07); add baselines/ablation/scaling/attribution (SYS-07/08) |
| DISC rules (guideline 05) | Evaluation §"Discussion and limitations" or short pre-Related section | Mirror logic and echo language apply; a standalone Discussion section is optional |
| CONC rules (guideline 06) | Conclusion | Apply; ignore the 100–200-word journal norm — a systems conclusion is typically one short paragraph to a third of a page |
| ABST/TITLE (guidelines 07/08) | Abstract/Title | Apply, specialized by SYS-11 |
| PARA/LINK/LANG (guidelines 09/10) | Everywhere | Apply unchanged |
| FIG/CIT (guideline 11) | Everywhere | Apply; add data-figure expectation (SYS-10) |

## 4. Systems-paper completion checklist

- [ ] Section skeleton matches SYS-01 (or a documented target-paper variant).
- [ ] Introduction has 3–5 verifiable contribution bullets and a numeric results preview.
- [ ] Motivation contains the authors' own measurements and ends in numbered requirements.
- [ ] An Overview section defines abstractions, architecture and a life-of-a-job walkthrough.
- [ ] There are 2–3 design sections, each traceable to a requirement, each insight-first.
- [ ] Implementation states stack and LOC; nothing conceptual hides there.
- [ ] Evaluation is RQ-driven with a full setup subsection; every RQ opens with its answer.
- [ ] Baselines include the nearest published competitor and a tuned naive approach.
- [ ] Ablations attribute the end-to-end gain to the individual design sections.
- [ ] Every speedup names baseline, endpoint and residency; negative results are reported.
- [ ] Related Work is near the end, organized by axis, citing measured comparisons where possible.
- [ ] Most figures are data figures; the Evaluation is the largest section.
- [ ] Claim→evidence matrix and artifact plan exist alongside the draft.
