# Paper Self-check List

## Full paper

- [ ] I can state the central claim in one sentence.
- [ ] The central claim corresponds to a clear achievement/contribution.
- [ ] The core words in the Title, Abstract, Discussion and Conclusion are consistent.
- [ ] The target readership, genre and target venue are defined.
- [ ] The section order serves the reader, not just replicating the experimental timeline.

## Systems paper structure (see guideline 14)

- [ ] The skeleton is Intro → Motivation → Overview → Design ×2–3 → Implementation → Evaluation → Related Work → Conclusion (or a documented target-paper variant).
- [ ] Contribution bullets ↔ design sections ↔ evaluation RQs form a one-to-one chain.
- [ ] Motivation is measured, and ends in numbered requirements.
- [ ] The Overview defines abstractions, component responsibilities and a life-of-a-job walkthrough.
- [ ] Every speedup names baseline, endpoint and residency; ablations attribute the gains.
- [ ] The Evaluation is the largest section and holds most of the data figures.

## Argument closure

- [ ] The gap in the Introduction has a handling plan in the Methods.
- [ ] Each objective/hypothesis has evidence in the Results.
- [ ] Each key result has an interpretation or comparison in the Discussion.
- [ ] Each main conclusion is constrained by evidence and study scope.
- [ ] Limitations correspond to future directions.

## Per-paragraph

- [ ] The paragraph has a single function.
- [ ] The paragraph starts with an entry sentence or a clear functional entry.
- [ ] There is no loose sentence inside the paragraph.
- [ ] The paragraph end carries the reader forward to the next paragraph.

## Per-sentence

- [ ] The role of each sentence can be named.
- [ ] Known information comes first, new information comes later.
- [ ] Claims and evidence are adjacent.
- [ ] The relationship between sentences is explicit and accurate.
- [ ] There is no dangling this/it/which.
- [ ] There is no unparseable long noun string, preposition string or -ing structure.

## Language

- [ ] Tense communicates fact, current relevance and stability.
- [ ] Passive sentences do not lose ownership of this paper's contribution.
- [ ] Modal verbs are consistent with evidence strength.
- [ ] Modal verbs avoid the high-risk traps: must (=certainty without evidence), can (three meanings), can not≠cannot, must not≠need not, should (two meanings).
- [ ] Causal verbs do not exceed the strength the design supports.
- [ ] Terminology is repeated and meaning is stable (no synonym swapping to avoid repetition).
- [ ] only, just, with, while, as, since etc. are not ambiguous.
- [ ] Sentence length and information density match the target articles.
- [ ] No sentence begins with an -ing form or a preposition.
- [ ] we refers to the authors of this paper, not the whole field.
- [ ] Hyphen position does not change the scope of modification.
- [ ] Easily confused word pairs (affect/effect, comprise/include, e.g./i.e. etc.) are used correctly.
- [ ] No unconscious lexical writing tics (indeed/in fact/basically/clearly).

## Numbers and evaluation

- [ ] Each key number has an evaluative direction (only/as high as/substantial etc.), with no "naked number".
- [ ] Frequency modifiers are consistent with the actually observed frequency and field expectation.
- [ ] The object, dimension and degree of comparison are all clear.
- [ ] Numbers in the abstract also have an evaluative direction.

## Figures and citations

- [ ] Each figure/table has an introduction, a reading guide and a result interpretation.
- [ ] The order of figures/tables serves the argument.
- [ ] Each citation is located next to the content it actually supports.
- [ ] Existing methods in the Methods have a citation and the necessary minimum description.
- [ ] Supplementary material has a specific locator.
