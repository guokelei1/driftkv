# Paragraph and Sentence Role Template

> Applies unchanged to systems-conference papers. Additional systems-specific paragraph openers
> worth having in the toolbox: RQ-answer sentences ("SystemName reduces X by Y× (Figure n).") at
> the head of evaluation subsections, and insight sentences ("The key insight is that ...") at the
> head of design sections.

## 1. Write the paragraph function first

```text
What this paragraph must accomplish: ____________________
What the reader already knows when starting this paragraph: __________
What the reader should newly know when finishing this paragraph: ________
The entry point for the next paragraph is: ______________
```

## 2. Sentence arrangement

| Position | Sentence function | Available start | What the sentence must provide | What the next sentence picks up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | entry/topic | `To address...` / `In general...` | Direction of the paragraph | Topic word |
| 2 | background/scope | `This...` / repeat topic word | Background the reader needs | New constraint/object |
| 3 | claim | `However...` / `Here...` | The core of what the author wants to say | Evidence |
| 4 | evidence | `Figure...` / `We found...` | Data, literature or steps | Evaluation |
| 5 | evaluation/interpretation | `Importantly...` / `This suggests...` | Significance, strength or mechanism | Limitation/transition |
| 6 | limitation/transition | `Although...` / `Taken together...` | Boundary and next direction | Known starting point for next paragraph |

## 3. Four sentence-linking options

1. Repeat an important word;
2. `This/These + specific noun`;
3. Use a semicolon to join highly related short sentences;
4. Use an accurate therefore/however/moreover/for example.

Choose the logical relationship first, then the linking device. Do not add a signal at the start of every sentence just to "have a connector".

## 4. Per-sentence self-check

- If this sentence is deleted, what is missing from the paragraph?
- Can the subject of this sentence really perform the verb?
- Does the reader know what this/it/which refers to?
- Will the new information in this sentence become the known information in the next sentence?
- Does only/just/simply/with/while in the sentence change the scope or causation?
- Is this an observation, an interpretation, a comparison, a speculation or a conclusion? Do the verb and tense match?
